# **1.Before starting in the lab**

Put your sample labels in the csv plate layout template, it is easiest
to only change the sample names and adapt the rest in the dPCR software.

Template can be found here:
[[https://drive.google.com/file/d/1OkCAZvAU1gwobCJHpbfCX4KBxyMZYobf/view?usp=sharing]{.underline}](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1OkCAZvAU1gwobCJHpbfCX4KBxyMZYobf/view?usp=sharing)

# **2.Preparations dPCR**

1.  Check which dilutions to use for your samples to get the best
    results

2.  Prepare primers and probes

## **2.1 Test which dilution works best for your samples**

This only needs to be done once at the start of a new project and does
not need to be repeated for each dPCR. When you have found the dilution
that work for your samples you can continue using these.

**Use dilutions of your samples:**

Genes: original sample, 1:10, 1:100

16S: 1:100, 1:500, 1:1000, 1:10.000, 1:100.000

10 uL of your original sample can be put into the first tube of a PCR
strip.

1:10 dilution: 1 uL sample + 9 uL PCR clean H2O

1:100 dilution: 1 uL 1:10 dilution + 9 uL PCR clean H2O

1:500 dilution: 2 uL 1:100 dilution +8 uL PCR clean H2O

Etc. (Alternatively you can use 2 uL of sample and double the amount of
PCR clean H2O).

It is best to make fresh dilutions on the same day to ensure correct
results (especially dilutions \> 1:1000). Vortex very well (10 sec) in
before pipetting to the next dilution. Use 0.2 ml strips.

*Hint: For kit extracted samples 1:1000 was used for 16S (and manure
samples) and 1(undiluted) sample for all other genes. For
phenol-chloroform extractions* *: dilution 1:1000 for 16S rRNA and
manure samples and dilution 1:10 combined with BSA for intI1 and the
other resistance genes.*

## **2.2. Prepare primers and probes**

All of our diluted primers have a concentration of 10 uM, the undiluted
primer stock has a concentration of 100uM and will be labeled as such.
If you have to dilute from the stock follow this example:

Concentration primer 100 uM

1:10 dilution: 1 uL 100uM primer/probe + 9uL PCR clean H2O or
alternatively:

2.  uL 100uM primer/probe + 18uL PCR clean H2O

# **3.Prepare MasterMix (volumes for 8.5k 96 well plates)**

Total volume per well is 12 uL, this includes 2 uL of DNA template (so
you need 10 uL of master mix per well). Times this to the number of
wells you are planning to fill including some extras and at least one
negative. For each gene you are testing, you need to make a separate
mastermix. Make sure that for the total volume you have round numbers or
a pipette that can pipette the right volumes. 

**When using phenol-chloroform extracted samples, BSA is added to the
mastermix, and less RNase free water is added.**

When you are pipetting a full plate, you can pipette your mastermix into
a PCR strip to make it easier to use the multichannel pipette.

# **4.Prepare plate**

1.  Decontaminate UV bench for at least 10 minutes with air circulation
    and H2O2.

2.  Thaw completely, vortex, and spin down everything you will use.
    Note: probe is not light stable, keep it in the dark. Put everything
    in fridge or cooling block when you're not using it. 

3.  Take a 96 PCR clean plate, only open in UV bench.

4.  Pipette your Mastermix into PCR strip for multichannel pipetting.

5.  Take a dPCR plate, cover film, and plate holder. Don't touch the
    underside of the plate. Put the plate in plate holder and cover the
    plate by laying the film on top of the plate.

6.  Pipette in 10 uL of mastermix into each well of the 96 PCR clean
    plate.

7.  Then pipette your DNA into the mastermix, per column.

    - Pipette up and down (5x), and pipette 10 uL into the dPCR plate
      without any bubbles. Do not press until second pressure point of
      the pipette. Pipette slowly, at a 45 degree angle, into the plate
      but without touching the bottom of the plate. 

    - Include at least one blank per gene with only mastermix.

8.  Stick on foil:

- Pull off the white part of the cover film, lay the blue part in the
  middle of the plate

- Use the roller from dPCR drawers to secure it.

- Tighten with your fingers. Take off clear foil while holding down the
  blue film with your hands

- Use a lot of pressure to roll down the blue film, until you can see
  the structure of the plate, also roll on edges. Don't move the plate
  while doing this. 

General:

- Mastermix can be stored in the fridge for a few weeks but is better
  stored in freezer when you are not using it often. 

**5.dPCR machine**

1.  Start up the laptop next to the dPCR machine. Password laptop =
    [A]{.underline}dmin

2.  Open the Qiacuity software suite (on desktop). You will get a
    warming message that the connection is not private. Go to advanced
    \> proceed to local host (unsafe)

3.  You can login to the software and the machine using login = admin,
    and password = admin.

4.  Create a new plate for each experiment (can be made into a template
    too, to reuse). 

5.  Go through steps for the plate

    - Name plate: include your name, gene names, sample names (optional
      dilutions)

    - Select 8.5K, 96 well plate

    - Description/ labels/ plate ownership can be skipped

    - Make sure you use the right plates! 96 well plates are black and
      gray (not blue!)

    - Scan barcode of plate (on side). Find barcode scanner in drawers
      below dPCR machine and connect via usb to the laptop. Scan side of
      plate until you hear a beep. Remember which plate you scanned for
      which digital plate if you are working with more than one plate
      (or number your physical plates). 

    - Put scanner away.

    - Safe changes.

**6.Load plate**

1.  Log into the machine.

2.  Open the tray by pressing the button.

3.  Put in plate, with barcode facing the back of the machine.

4.  You can see that the plate is loaded and you can press play/ run all

## **Critical Control Points**

⚠ Step: Pipetting without bubbles

Why it is critical: It will make it impossible for the machine to
analyse your samples when there are air bubbles.

How to avoid mistakes: For the first time do this together with someone
who has done it before. Practice the pipetting before starting and work
carefully/ slowly.
